Kalakraft · Admin · Mobile · Proposal ii

Stop drawing an app. Draw an instrument.

The screens that shipped work. They do not read like software you would trust with a catalogue — they read like a consumer app: one blunt corner radius, grey blobs, bold everywhere, and figures that never line up. This proposal keeps every function and changes the material: hairlines instead of fills, one column for every number, type that stops shouting, and a radius ladder that is soft without being shapeless.

The diagnosis

Seven reasons it reads cheap

Each row states the cause, then shows the same element as it ships today and as proposed. Both specimens are live HTML at the real sizes — nothing here is a mockup of a mockup.

One radius, and it is the largest one

16 pt on a list, 13 on a 52 pt thumbnail, 11 on a chip — no ladder, and the biggest value landing on the smallest elements. Roundness is not the problem; a single undifferentiated radius is. Four steps — 8 / 10 / 12 / 18 — stay soft and still give the eye a hierarchy.

The direction

Six rules, and the whole thing falls out of them

R1

Hairlines, not fills

Grey is a 1 px rule. Surfaces stay white. The only filled shape on a screen is the one thing you press.

R2

Every figure gets a column

Quantities are tabular, right-aligned, on one axis, under a labelled header. This single move is the difference between a list and a ledger.

R3

A radius ladder, 8 · 10 · 12 · 18

Thumbnails and chips 8, controls 10, tiles and containers 12, sheets and the dock 18. Soft enough to read as current software, stepped so size still means something. Pills are for the 5 px status dot only.

R4

Four sizes, three weights

10 mono / 12 / 14 / 16, at 400·500·600. One 600 per region. Labels are mono uppercase; values are sans.

R5

Colour is a signal

Desaturated amber and red, on the quantity only, with a 5 px dot. Never on a label, never as a filled chip.

R6

Chrome sits flat

No blur, no shadow, no floating cards. Bars are white with one hairline. Sheets are the only elevated surface.

Live — tap anything

  1. 01

    The app bar is the title, and it holds the verb

    The 30-point in-page heading goes. The shared bar already says Products; printing it twice cost 54 pt and made the screen open on decoration. New becomes a quiet + in the bar, so the one creative action is reachable at any scroll position instead of scrolling away.

  2. 02

    Search and filter, then the facets, as one sticky control surface

    A 38 pt hairline field, a 38 pt filter button that wears a dot when a category is on, and under them the four counts as an underline strip — the stat cards, spent. It is 78 pt of chrome that never moves, drawn flat, with a single rule separating it from the data.

  3. 03

    A column header — and it is also the sort control

    PRODUCT on the left, the sort key on the right. Tap it and the sheet offers stock, price, units sold, name, recently updated — and the right-hand column becomes whatever you chose. Sort by best-selling and the figures turn into units sold. One control, one column, no duplicated state.

  4. 04

    57-point rows, 36-point thumbnails, one figure on the right

    Ten rows on a screen instead of seven. The second line is mono: index, category and the fact the sort column is not showing. Hidden products mute their name and dim the photo — no coloured chip needed. The divider insets to the text edge, so the column of thumbnails reads as a column.

  5. 05

    The row sheet leads with stock, because that is the phone job

    Nobody rewrites product copy on a phone; they correct a count after a delivery. So the sheet opens with a stepper on the number itself, then visibility, then edit, storefront and delete. Adjusting stock never leaves the list.

  6. 06

    Select mode, for when it is not one product

    The header's Select turns the rows into checkboxes and raises a flat bar: publish, hide, delete, with a live count. It is the feature that separates a catalogue admin from a list — and it makes the single-row case cheap too, because the same bar handles one.

LIVE ON THE STOREFRONT

Live — the dock is gone on purpose

  1. 01

    The create flow hides the dock, the way checkout does

    It is a task, not a destination. AppRootClient already drops the dock on auth and checkout routes; adding this one lets the action bar sit flush on the bottom edge instead of floating above a floating pill — which is the single biggest reason the current screen reads as an app rather than a tool.

  2. 02

    Steps as underline tabs, with a 2-point progress rule above them

    The four black pills go. Mono uppercase labels, a 5 pt dot that fills green when the step is satisfied, and a 1.5 pt underline on the one you are in. The rule above tracks required progress across the full bleed. Total chrome: 46 pt, against 78.

  3. 03

    Fields are rules, not boxes

    A mono uppercase label, the value at 16 pt with no border, and a hairline beneath. Focus darkens that hairline to near-black and lifts the label with it — the field's own rule carries the state, so nothing new appears. It reads as a record being filled in, and it stops every input looking like the button you are meant to press.

  4. 04

    Structured values are 52-point rows

    Currency, category, publish: label left, value right, chevron, hairline. The same row shape the row sheet uses, so the whole product speaks one grammar. Pickers are sheets — never a portal popover on a phone.

  5. 05

    Price is the one large figure on the screen

    26 pt, weight 500, tabular, with the symbol as a muted prefix. Nothing else on the step competes with it. Stock sits under it as a stepper, because it is a count you nudge, not a number you type.

  6. 06

    Photos are numbered, and the cover is a border

    Six-point radius, hairline, a mono index in the corner, and the cover marked by a 1.5 pt near-black border plus a small COVER tag — not a filled black pill. The remove control is a 22 pt square that is always there, and tapping a tile opens make-cover / move / remove.

  7. 07

    Review is a summary, not a product card

    A definition list: name, price, stock, category, photos, tags — values right-aligned, missing ones in red with the row as the way to go set them. The publish switch, then Create. It reads like a record about to be committed, which is what it is.

  8. 08

    Blocked says why, in the bar

    A hairline strip above the buttons naming the missing field and the step that holds it, tappable. Kept from the current build — it was the one thing there that already behaved like grown-up software.

The shadcn layer

Most of this is a token change, not a rewrite

The look above is not a departure from shadcn — it is shadcn with its dials moved. Three of these are one-line edits in globals.css that would lift every admin page at once, not only these two.

Primitives worth fixing

InputDrop shadow-sm — a shadow on a text field is a 2020 look — and take rounded-md from the new ladder, so it lands at 10 rather than 8. Add a variant="line" for the rule-separated fields above.
ButtonAdd size="xs" at 28 pt; the dashboard fakes it with h-6 today. Drop shadow-sm from outline. Heights move to 42 / 40 / 36 so the ladder and the touch floor agree.
CardRetire shadow-sm in the dashboard; a hairline is the elevation. Shadows are for sheets only, from --shadow-sheet.
BadgeCurrently only pill-shaped. Add a squared rounded-sm variant — a filled pill is what the status chips got wrong.
SheetFold the grabber, the head row and the dismiss word in, so ActionSheet stops re-implementing them. Corners to xl, and a 1.5 px scrim blur behind.
SwitchReads --primary when on, so "live" and "publish" are near-black. Point it at --success: the state is affirmative, not primary.
SonnerToasts land under the dock on these screens. One offset tied to --mobile-dock-total fixes it everywhere at once.
globals.cssThe mobile touch-target block is attribute selectors inside max-width: 767.98px. It should be control-height tokens, so 768–1023 pt gets them too.

Before anything is built

Five things only you can answer

  • Q1

    Is there a SKU, or should there be?

    The row's second line wants a stable handle. Product has no SKU field, so the prototype shows #14 — the id. A real sku column would be a small migration and would make this screen, the restock queue and any future export read the way stock software is expected to.

  • Q2

    Select mode — in, or later?

    It needs bulk endpoints (PATCH many, DELETE many) or a loop on the client. It is the largest single addition here and the one that most makes the screen feel like inventory software. It can also ship second, without redrawing anything.

  • Q3

    May the create flow hide the dock?

    One line in AppRootClient, alongside the auth and checkout exclusions. It is what lets the action bar sit flush, and it would apply to editing a product too.

  • Q4

    Do the tokens move for the whole dashboard, or only here?

    Changing --radius, the neutrals and the semantics lifts orders, users, coupons, reviews and support in the same commit — and leaves them consistent. Scoping it to these two screens is possible but guarantees drift.

  • Q5

    Rules or boxes for the form?

    The rule-separated field is the boldest call in this document. It reads far more expensive; it is also less obviously tappable than a bordered box. The middle road — hairline boxes at radius 6 with the mono labels — gets most of the gain with none of the risk. Say which and I will draw it that way.
