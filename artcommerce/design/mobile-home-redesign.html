01 — You asked me to check first

Is the bottom navigation universal?

Yes — and it has been for a while.

MobileDock renders from AppRootClient on every route except auth and checkout, which are deliberately excluded as single-purpose flows. Its own comment says it replaced two systems: a hamburger drawer on the storefront and a full-width tab bar in the dashboard, which used to swap under you at the /dashboard boundary. So this isn't a question of making it universal. It's a question of whether three targets — Home, Search, Cart — are the right three for a marketplace.

What it does today

  • Floating pill, max-w-sm, blurred, above the safe area
  • Home is context-aware — it becomes Dashboard inside /dashboard
  • Search opens the command palette, which searches products, pages and actions together
  • Cart carries a live count and routes to /dashboard/cart when signed in
  • Hides on scroll down past 120px, returns on scroll up

What a marketplace misses

  • No browse target. Discovery is search-only, and search needs a word you already have. Shopping is often "show me what you have".
  • No saved items. Wishlist exists in the schema and the dashboard, but is unreachable from the dock.
  • No account. Only reachable through Home once you're already in the dashboard.
  • The first target is unstable — the same position means Home or Dashboard depending on where you stand.

Three ways to spend the space

Switch between them on the live phone below — the dock is real.

A — Keep three

No change. The homepage's own category rail carries browsing. Cheapest, and the dock stays uncluttered — but "saved" stays invisible, and search keeps carrying discovery it isn't good at.

B — Four, with search moved up (recommended)

Home · Shop · Saved · Cart. Search becomes a real field pinned to the top of the home screen, where a thumb looks for it while browsing, and the freed slot buys a browse destination. The palette still opens from the field.

C — Five

Home · Shop · Search · Saved · Cart. Everything addressable, nothing hidden — but five targets in a floating pill at 390px leaves each about 62px, and the labels start to crowd.

04 — Findings

Six things to change on the way

01

Nothing to search with

There is no search field on the page. The only way in is the dock's palette, behind an icon, which auto-hides as you scroll down.

A field at the top, sticky

It looks like an input and opens the palette you already built. Costs 42px and is the first thing a shopper reaches for.

02

Categories are hardcoded and fuzzy

Nine categories are written into page.tsx and matched by substring against category names. Two of them — "Trays" (tray) and "Jewelry Trays" (Tray) — differ only in case, so they return the same products. Meanwhile /api/categories returns the real list, unused.

Read the real categories

Rail sourced from /api/categories, linking to /products?category=<slug>, which the products API already filters on properly.

03

The grid reshuffles every visit

Products are sorted with Math.random() and sliced to eight. Come back tomorrow and the piece you half-remember is gone — the worst property a storefront can have.

Ordered by something meaningful

Best sellers from /api/products/best-sellers, new arrivals from the default createdAt desc. Stable, explicable, and already built.

04

Four carousels of the same shape

Our Collection, Best Sellers, Featured Discoveries and Explore Our Artisan Creations are all horizontal scrollers of product images. By the third, they stop reading as different things.

Vary the shape, not just the title

One ranked rail, one 2-up grid, one editorial band. Different shapes tell you something different is being said.

05

Decoration between every section

Watercolour splashes, ink splashes, brush accents, light effects and header rules sit between sections, adding height without adding information.

Let the products be the colour

Section headers are a title and a "See all". The pieces are vivid enough — resin art doesn't need a painted frame around it.

06

No price, rating or stock on the card

The legacy cards show a name and a price. The API already returns rating average and count, and stockQuantity is right there — none of it reaches the card.

A card that answers the obvious questions

Price with any MRP struck through, rating, a scarcity or made-to-order flag, a save heart and one-tap add. Anatomy below.

05 — Where each band gets its data

Everything here is already queryable

No new endpoints. The products API already takes category, usageTag, sort, inStock, priceMin/Max and ratingMin, and returns rating averages and counts alongside each product.

Card anatomy

  • Square image, aspect-square, 13px radius — one shape everywhere
  • Heart top-right, 32px visual in a 44px hit area
  • Name clamped to two lines so cards stay the same height
  • Price 13.5px semibold; MRP struck through only when there is one
  • Rating right-aligned on the price row, hidden when there are no reviews
  • Rank pill only in the best-sellers rail, where the number means something

Performance

  • Above the fold is the top bar and the first hero slide — everything else lazy
  • Rails render 6 cards, not 20; the rest is a "See all"
  • Cloudinary crops per slot (w_320 for cards, w_780 for hero) rather than one large asset reused
  • The legacy page ships four carousels and a video on first load — this drops the video to the collection page

Accessibility

  • Rails are horizontally scrollable with visible focus, not drag-only
  • Hearts are aria-pressed toggles with a label naming the piece
  • Section headers are real headings so the page can be skimmed by structure
  • Dock targets keep 44px and gain visible labels in options B and C

06 — Before I build

Five things only you can answer

1 · Which dock?B is my recommendation — Home · Shop · Saved · Cart, with search promoted to the top bar. It changes navigation app-wide, not just here, so it's your call.
2 · Does "Shop" go to /products or a category index?Straight to the products list is simpler; a category index screen browses better when you have more than a dozen categories.
3 · Is there an MRP to strike through?The schema has one price. Struck-through pricing needs a compare-at field, or the cards drop it.
4 · Quick add, or always open the product?No variants means one-tap add works — but handcrafted pieces sometimes want the detail page read first.
5 · Keep the video?It's a real asset, just an expensive first-load. I'd move it to the collection page rather than delete it.
