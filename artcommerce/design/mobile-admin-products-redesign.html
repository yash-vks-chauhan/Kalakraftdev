Kalakraft · Admin · Mobile rebuild

The catalogue, rebuilt for the hand that runs it.

Two admin screens carry the whole catalogue — the list you manage it from, and the form you add to it with. Both were designed at 1440 pt and folded down. This is what they become when the phone is the brief, not the leftover.

01 — The audit

What a 390-point screen does to these two pages

Not "it looks cramped". Each of these is a specific thing an admin cannot do, or pays for, on a phone today — traced back to the rule in the code that causes it.

Screen one

/dashboard/admin/products

  • ₹

    Half the first screen is numbers you cannot act on

    Four stat cards stack 2×2 above everything. Roughly 160 pt of the opening view goes to Total / Active / Low stock / Out — and none of them is tappable, so knowing six items are low does not get you to those six items.

    grid-cols-2 · four Card + CardContent p-4 · no href
  • ₹

    Search is below the fold, on the one screen that needs it most

    The field sits inside the list card, after the stats and the category rail — roughly 300 pt down. With a catalogue of any size, the first thing you reach for is the thing you have to scroll to.

    CardHeader > Input, after the stats grid and the rail
  • ₹

    Three frames wrap every row

    Page padding, then a bordered Card, then the list's own padding. A row gets about 326 pt of a 390 pt screen, and the card outline earns none of it — it draws a box around something already the width of the phone.

    px-4 → Card border → CardContent → li px-4
  • ₹

    Hiding one product costs three taps and shows no state

    The desktop table gives status its own column with a switch. The phone card drops it: nothing on the row says live or hidden, and changing it means ⋯ → sheet → tap.

    Switch column is inside DesktopTableFrame only
  • ₹

    Categories arrive as a sideways scroll

    Each rail button carries an icon, a label and a count badge, so two and a half fit across. Filtering by a category near the end of the list means swiping a strip to find it.

    nav overflow-x-auto, md:flex-col
  • ·

    Two catalogues of the same rows are in the DOM

    The desktop table is rendered and hidden with hidden md:block; the card list is rendered and hidden with md:hidden. A phone parses a six-column table it will never paint.

    DesktopTableFrame + DataCardList, both mounted
  • ·

    "Low stock" means two different numbers

    This page calls ten or fewer low; the dashboard's own alert counts five or fewer. Same words, two answers, no way to tell which one you are reading.

    LOW_STOCK_THRESHOLD = 10 · api/admin/metrics = 5

Screen two

/dashboard/admin/products/new

  • ✕

    You cannot remove a photo you picked by mistake

    The tile's × and its drag grip are opacity-0 until group-hover. A touch screen never hovers, so both controls are invisible — the only way to reach the × is to tab a focus ring onto it.

    opacity-0 group-hover:opacity-100 focus:opacity-100
  • ✕

    You cannot choose which photo is the cover

    Order is the cover — first image wins — and reordering is HTML5 draggable with onDragStart/onDrop. Touch does not fire those events, so on a phone the cover is whichever file the picker handed over first.

    draggable + onDragStart/onDragOver/onDrop
  • ✕

    You cannot save it as a draft

    The Visible / Draft switch lives in the command bar behind hidden sm:flex, and it is the only isActive control on the page. Below 640 pt every product you create publishes to the storefront immediately.

    div className="hidden … sm:flex" wrapping the only Switch
  • ✕

    The page's command bar rides over the app's header

    Both are sticky top-0 z-30. They claim the same offset and the same layer, so the later one in the DOM — the page's — paints across the header, its back chevron and the account button.

    MobileHeader sticky top-0 z-30 · command bar sticky top-0 z-30
  • ₹

    The live preview is a screen-and-a-half below the field you are typing in

    Preview and checklist are the lg right column. Below lg they fall past the entire form — two cards nobody scrolls to, describing what is on screen above them.

    aside lg:sticky, stacked after the form on mobile
  • ₹

    The four tabs do not fit, so they scroll under a sliding thumb

    Icon, label and padding put the row near 410 pt inside 358 pt of screen. Discovery starts off-screen; the animated indicator measures against a container that is itself moving.

    SegmentedControl overflow-x-auto, four icon+label items
  • ₹

    Step one is the longest step, and most of it is optional

    Name, slug, short description, description, specifications and care — six fields, two of them tall optional textareas, roughly 940 pt of column. The required fields are buried by the ones nobody fills on a phone.

    BasicsFields: 4 inputs + 3 textareas, all expanded
  • ·

    The copy is written for a mouse

    "Drag product images here, or click to browse" describes a gesture the device does not have, on the step where the phone is actually the better tool — the photos are already on it.

    dropzone label copy
Broken — the task cannot be completed on a phone Costly — possible, but it charges taps or screen for nothing Noise

02 — The rules

Five decisions everything below follows from

i

One surface, hairlines inside

No card inside a card. A list is a single filled block divided by rules, the way the mobile Overview already does it — the phone edge is the frame.

ii

A number you cannot tap is decoration

The four stat cards become the four filters. Same counts, a quarter of the height, and now "6 low" is the way to reach those six.

iii

Nothing behind hover

Every control is visible at rest and at least 44 pt. Reordering is a tap and a named action, never a drag the OS will not report.

iv

One question per screen

The form asks four things in turn. What is it, what does it cost, what does it look like, how is it found — and the fifth screen is the one where you confirm.

v

The verb stays under the thumb

Continue, and later Create, live in a bar above the dock — the same place and the same shape as the cart's checkout card, so the app has one answer for "the action".

  1. 1

    The title is the page's, and the bar borrows it

    "Products" is set at 30 pt in the content with the live count underneath, and the app bar stays empty until it scrolls past — the arrangement the Overview already uses. New sits beside it as a filled 40 pt pill, so the one creative action on the screen reads as the one dark thing on it.

  2. 2

    Search first, and it stays

    The field is the second thing on the page instead of the seventh. Beside it, one 44 pt button opens everything else — category, order, the two sibling pages — and wears a dot when a refinement is on, so state survives scrolling out of view.

  3. 3

    The stat cards became the filter rail

    All · Live · Hidden · Low · Out, each carrying the count that used to sit in a card. 160 pt of read-only tiles becomes a 36 pt rail where every number is also the route to the products behind it. Low spells out its threshold in the sheet, so "low" stops meaning two things.

  4. 4

    One block, hairlines, 74-point rows

    No card, no border, no nesting — the row runs the width of the phone. A 52 pt photo, the name, then price and stock on one meta line with a severity dot. Hidden products dim their photo and say so; sold-through says "Out of stock" in red where the count would be.

  5. 5

    Tap the row to edit, tap ⋯ for the rest

    Editing is what you came for, so it is the whole row. The 46 pt ⋯ column holds hide/publish, storefront and delete — each a 54 pt target with a label, so nothing is guessed from an icon.

  6. 6

    Refine is one sheet, not three controls

    Category as a checked list with counts, sort order underneath, and the two sibling pages — Highest rated, and the restock queue — as jumps at the bottom. It replaces a rail that never fit and adds an order control the page never had.

  1. 1

    Four steps that fit, and say where you are

    Short labels, no icons: Basics · Price · Photos · Tags across 368 pt with room to spare. Each carries a ring that fills green when its step is satisfied, and a hairline under the rail tracks required progress. The rail replaces the page's own command bar entirely, so nothing competes with the app header for top: 0.

  2. 2

    Basics asks four things; the other two are folded

    Name, the URL it generates, the short description with its counter, and the full description. Specifications and care collapse into disclosure rows that report their own state — "Not added", "142 characters" — so the optional prose stops burying the required fields.

  3. 3

    The slug is a line of text until you want it

    It writes itself from the name and shows as /products/… under the field, with Edit beside it. The composite input with the prefix inside the border only appears if you ask for it — which on a phone is almost never.

  4. 4

    Price is set at 19 points, currency is three buttons

    A numeric keypad field with the symbol inside it, and INR/USD/EUR as a segmented control — three options do not deserve a portal and a scroll. Stock gets −/+ around the number, because on a phone adjusting by two should not mean selecting text.

  5. 5

    Photos: every control visible, reorder by naming it

    A 26 pt × sits on every tile at rest. Tapping a tile opens Make cover · Move earlier · Move later · Remove, and the tile numbers itself so order is legible without dragging. Uploads take their own tile with a percentage. The dashed zone reads "Add photos — camera or library", which is what the device actually offers.

  6. 6

    Review is the confirmation, and it can be opened from anywhere

    One sheet holds what the desktop keeps in its right column: the live preview card, the checklist as jump targets, the publish switch that the phone did not have at all — and the Create button. It is the last screen before the product exists, which is the right place to decide whether it goes live.

  7. 7

    The bar states the blocker instead of greying out in silence

    When Create cannot fire, the strip above it names what is missing — "Needs a price · Price" — and tapping it goes to that step. A disabled button that explains nothing is the commonest way a mobile form loses someone.

07 — What ships

Two trees, one source of state

The same approach the Overview took: pick the tree with useIsDesktop() rather than rendering both and hiding one. The desktop layout is not edited — it moves into its own file and keeps its markup.

  • products/page.tsxFetching, handlers and the desktop tree; picks the mobile tree below lg.
  • products/_components/ProductsMobile.tsxScreen one — title, search, status rail, hairline list, refine sheet.
  • products/new/page.tsxThin switch between the two trees.
  • products/new/_lib/useNewProductForm.tsAll state, uploads, validation and submit — shared, so the trees cannot drift.
  • products/new/_components/NewProductDesktop.tsxToday's layout, moved verbatim.
  • products/new/_components/NewProductMobile.tsxScreen two — step rail, four steps, action bar, review sheet.
  • dashboard/_components/nav-config.tsProducts gets collapsingTitle, so the app bar defers to the page's own title.
