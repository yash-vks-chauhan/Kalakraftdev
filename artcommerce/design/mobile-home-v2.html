Mobile · the whole page, top to bottom

Poured, not printed.

The first pass got the marketplace structure right and left it looking like every other marketplace. This one keeps that structure and gives it a point of view: shadcn primitives carry every piece of UI — quiet, systematic, professional — and the artistry is one device used exactly twice, a slow resin pour behind the hero and behind the footer, bookending the page. Serif speaks only in the brand's voice. Everything else is Inter doing its job. Scroll the phone.

01

An empty right side

The header is a three-column layout where the centre and right columns render nothing on the homepage. That's about 250 px of a 70 px-tall fixed bar doing no work on every screen.

Saved and account, where thumbs expect them

Two 40 px targets on the right — the two destinations the dock can't hold. Cart and search stay in the dock, so nothing is duplicated.

02

A logo image where a wordmark belongs

Mobile ships a 110×36 PNG. Desktop already renders a serif wordmark in the brand's voice — styles.wordmark — and mobile keeps the raster.

The same serif wordmark

Text, not an image: sharp at any density, themeable, weightless, and it makes the two viewports look like one brand.

03

A scroll state you can't see

rgba(255,255,255,0.1) plus blur over a near-white page is barely a change. The bar and the content underneath it stay ambiguous.

Transparent, then decisively solid

Over the hero it's fully transparent with white content. Past the hero it becomes an 86% frosted surface with a hairline border and dark content. Two clear states, nothing in between.

04

70 px, always

Fixed height regardless of context, and no search anywhere on the page.

Search rides along, then folds away

At rest: a 56 px row plus a 42 px search field. On scroll the field collapses to zero and reappears as an icon — the bar settles at 56 px, 14 px shorter than today while carrying three more controls.

02 — The whole page

One scroll, hero to footer

Scroll it. The navbar changes state past the hero, the pour keeps moving behind it, hearts and add buttons respond, the footer accordion opens and the newsletter accepts an address. The controls below jump you around and let you switch the grain and the motion off to see what each is doing.

The three artistic devicesused sparingly

The pour

Four blurred colour fields drifting on a 26-second loop, behind the hero and behind the footer — nowhere else. It's the product's own process as the page's only ambient motion.

Grain

An SVG turbulence layer at low opacity over every art surface. It's what stops a CSS gradient reading as a CSS gradient and starts it reading as pigment. Toggle it off on the phone — the page immediately looks cheaper.

Serif, rationed

Cormorant Garamond italic — already loaded by app/layout.tsx — appears in the wordmark, the last word of each section title, and the footer signature. Nowhere in the UI. Rarity is what keeps it feeling like a voice rather than a font choice.

What it isn't

No parallax, no scroll-jacking, no reveal-on-scroll for every card, no glassmorphism beyond the two surfaces that need it. The restraint is the professionalism.

03 — The system underneath

What shadcn actually buys here

You have nineteen primitives installed and the mobile homepage uses none of them — it's CSS modules with hand-rolled cards, pills and carousels. Moving to the primitives isn't cosmetic: it's how the radius, focus rings, disabled states and dark mode stop being decided page by page.

Three commands, once

npx shadcn@latest add accordion carousel aspect-ratio — that's the entire dependency cost of this design. Carousel brings Embla, which is what gives the hero real momentum and snap on touch instead of a scroll container pretending.

Tokens, not values

  • Radius: cards at calc(var(--radius) + 4px), controls at --radius, pills at full
  • One elevation, used on the dock and the toast only
  • Type: 4 sizes on this page — 38 / 19 / 14 / 12.5 — and two weights
  • Spacing: 16px page gutter, 12–14px between cards, 26px between sections

Dark mode, for free

The page is built on --background / --foreground rather than white and near-black, so the day the site gets a dark theme this page already has one. The legacy module CSS hardcodes #f8f8f8 and would need rewriting.

04 — You said do as I like

So here are the five answers

These were the open questions from the first pass. Each is now a decision, with the reasoning — overrule any of them and I'll rebuild that piece.

1 · Dock: four targets Home · Shop · Saved · Cart, search promoted to the header. Five is too many at 390px; three leaves browsing to a search box. Four is the only option where every target is a place rather than an action.
2 · Shop goes to /products Not a category index screen. You have nine categories. An index screen for nine items is a menu that exists to hold a menu — the home rail already does that job.
3 · No struck-through pricing One price, shown once. The schema has a single price. Faking an MRP to strike through is the oldest trick in retail and the wrong signal for handmade work — the price is the price.
4 · Quick add stays With a toast that offers the cart, and the card still opens the product. CartItem is product + quantity, no variants, so one tap is a complete and reversible action. The toast is the undo path.
5 · The video moves Off the homepage, onto the craft story page. It's a good asset in the wrong place: autoplaying video is the single most expensive thing on a first mobile load, and it sits below four carousels nobody reached.
Bonus · The hero is not a carousel One statement, two buttons, the pour behind it. I proposed three rotating slides last time and I think that was wrong. Rotating heroes split the message and measure badly; a single confident statement is what "highly professional" actually looks like. Carousel is still added — for the collection rails.
